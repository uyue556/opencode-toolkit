# Email Marketing Reference

Design and audit email sequences across the full lifecycle: welcome, nurture, onboarding, retention, billing, usage, win-back, and campaigns.

## 1. Core Principles

1. **One email, one job** — one primary purpose, one main CTA per email.
2. **Value before ask** — lead with usefulness; earn the right to sell.
3. **Relevance over volume** — fewer, better emails win; segment for relevance.
4. **Clear path forward** — every email moves the reader somewhere; make next steps obvious.

---

## 2. Sequence Strategy

**Length:** Welcome 3–7 · Lead nurture 5–10 · Onboarding 5–10 · Re-engagement 3–5. Depends on sales cycle, product complexity, relationship stage.

**Timing:** welcome immediate; early sequence 1–2 days apart; nurture 2–4 days; long-term weekly/bi-weekly. B2B avoid weekends; B2C test weekends; send at local time.

**Subject lines:** clear > clever; specific > vague; benefit or curiosity driven; 40–60 chars; test emoji (polarizing). Patterns that work:
- Question: "Still struggling with X?"
- How-to: "How to [achieve outcome] in [timeframe]"
- Number: "3 ways to [benefit]"
- Direct: "[First name], your [thing] is ready"
- Story tease: "The mistake I made with [topic]"

**Preview text:** ~90–140 chars, extends the subject, don't repeat it.

## 3. Sequence Templates

### Welcome (post-signup)
1. Welcome (immediate): deliver promised value, single next action.
2. Quick win (D1–2): "Get your first [result] in 10 minutes."
3. Story/why (D3–4): origin story, show you understand the problem.
4. Social proof (D5–6): case study/testimonial, soft CTA.
5. Overcome objection (D7–8): address a common hesitation.
6. Core feature (D9–11): underused capability, direct CTA to try.
7. Conversion (D12–14): summarize value, clear offer, urgency + risk reversal.

### Lead nurture (pre-sale)
Deliver + introduce → expand on topic → problem deep-dive → solution framework → case study → differentiation → objection handler → direct offer (D19–21).

### Re-engagement
1. Check-in (after 30–60 days inactivity): "Is everything okay?" genuine, easy win.
2. Value reminder: "Remember when you [achieved X]?"
3. Incentive (D5–7): limited-time offer.
4. Last chance (D10–14): "Should we stop emailing you?" — clean the list if no response.

### Onboarding (product users)
Coordinate with in-app; email supports, doesn't duplicate. Welcome + first step → getting-started help → feature highlight → success story → check-in (D7) → advanced tip (D10–12) → upgrade/expand (D14+).

## 4. Lifecycle Email Types

### Onboarding
- **New users** (5–7 emails/14 days): activate to aha moment; metrics = activation rate, feature adoption.
- **New customers** (3–5 emails/14 days): reinforce purchase, drive adoption, reduce churn — different from new-user series, they've committed.
- **Key step reminders:** "hasn't connected integration after 48h", "hasn't invited teammate after 3 days", "hasn't completed profile after 24h" — remind, explain why, direct link, offer help.
- **New user invite:** personalize with inviter's name; single accept CTA.

### Retention
- **Upgrade to paid:** triggers = time (trial D10/12/14), behavior (usage limit, premium feature), engagement (highly active free). Structure: value summary → feature comparison → social proof → urgency → last chance.
- **Upgrade to higher plan:** triggers = 80% seat limit, 90% storage, attempted higher-tier feature, power-user patterns. Positive framing, quantify value vs. cost.
- **Ask for review:** after positive support/milestone/renewal; never after billing issues or bugs; thank + mention specific value + explain why reviews matter + direct link.
- **Proactive support:** usage drop, failed actions, stuck at onboarding; "I noticed you…"; direct help, not docs-link spam; personal sender; no pitch.
- **Product usage report:** key metrics, comparison, achievements, suggestions; "You saved X hours", "You're in the top X%".
- **NPS survey:** one question first; follow-ups by score (promoter → thank + referral ask; passive → what would make it a 10; detractor → personal outreach).
- **Referral emails:** after NPS win, milestone, renewal; clear offer; unique link; show what's in it for both parties.

### Billing
- **Switch to annual:** exact savings, lock-in messaging, one-click; best near renewal, year-end, or price increase.
- **Failed payment recovery** (3–4 emails/7–14 days): assume accident (card expired); D0 friendly notice → D3 reminder (service may interrupt) → D7 urgent (suspension) → D10–14 final (what they'll lose). No guilt.
- **Cancellation survey:** primary reason, what could improve, what would change their mind; targeted save based on reason.
- **Upcoming renewal reminder:** date + amount, what's included, how to update, optional upsell; required for annual/high-value.

### Usage
- **Daily/weekly/monthly summaries:** must be relevant to actual usage — empty reports are worse than none.
- **Milestone notifications:** first [action], 10th/100th [thing], goal achieved, streak; celebration + what's next.

### Win-back
- **Expired trials** (3–4 emails/30 days): what you're missing → what held you back (feedback) → incentive → final reach-out. Segment by trial engagement.
- **Cancelled customers** (2–3 emails/90 days): D30 what's new → D60 "we've addressed [reason]" → D90 offer. No guilt; make return easy; more likely if their reason was addressed.

### Campaigns
- **Monthly roundup/newsletter:** consistent day/time, scannable, one primary CTA, unsubscribe is okay (keeps list healthy).
- **Seasonal promotions:** announcement → reminder → last chance.
- **Product updates:** what's new (simple) → why it matters (benefit) → how to use → who asked for it; target relevant segments/power users first.
- **Pricing update:** announce 30–60 days before, remind 14 days, final 7 days; transparent, explain why, grandfather if possible.

## 5. Audit Checklist

- Onboarding: new users, new customers, step reminders, invites.
- Retention: upgrade-to-paid, upsell triggers, review requests, proactive support, usage reports, NPS, referral.
- Billing: annual switch, failed payment recovery, cancellation survey, renewal reminders.
- Usage: summaries, key event notifications, milestone celebrations.
- Win-back: expired trials, cancelled customers.
- Campaigns: roundup, seasonal calendar, product updates, pricing comms.

## 6. Copy Guidelines

**Structure:** Hook → Context → Value → CTA → warm sign-off.
**Formatting:** short paragraphs (1–3 sentences), white space, bullets, sparing bold, mobile-first.
**Tone:** conversational, first/second person, active voice, human when read aloud.
**Length:** 50–125 words transactional · 150–300 educational · 300–500 story-driven (if long, be good).
**CTA:** buttons for primary actions; links for secondary; one primary CTA; button text = action + outcome.

## 7. Personalization & Segmentation

- **Merge fields:** first name (fallback "there"/"friend"), company (B2B), relevant data (usage/plan).
- **Dynamic content:** by segment, behavior, or stage.
- **Triggered emails:** action-based sends outperform time-based (feature used, milestone, inactivity).
- **Segment by behavior** (openers vs not, clickers vs not, active vs inactive), **by stage** (trial vs paid, engaged vs at-risk), **by profile** (industry/role, use case, company size).

---

## 8. Testing & Metrics

**Test:** subject lines (highest impact), send times, length, CTA placement/copy, personalization level, sequence timing. One variable at a time, sufficient sample, statistical significance, document learnings.

**Metrics & benchmarks:** open rate 20–40% · click rate 2–5% · unsubscribe <0.5% · conversion rate (sequence-specific) · revenue per email.
