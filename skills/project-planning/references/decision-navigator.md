# Decision Navigator

Guide stuck or overwhelmed users through targeted branching questions — one clear question at a time — until they reach concrete, actionable steps. Merged from: `decision-navigator`.

## When to Use

Use whenever a user feels stuck, overwhelmed, or doesn't know where to start. Trigger phrases: "I don't know what to do", "I want to X but don't know how", "I'm not sure where to begin", "help me figure out...", "I feel lost about...", or broad open-ended goals ("I want to start a business", "I want to change careers", "I need to make a decision about X").

Do not wait for a precise question. If they seem stuck, use this skill.

## Core Philosophy

Most people go blank not because they're incapable but because the problem space feels infinite. Collapse the space progressively: ask one clarifying question, offer 3-5 distinct paths, let them choose, repeat — getting more specific each level — until you reach a leaf where concrete steps make sense. Navigate, don't lecture.

## The Process

### Step 1 — Acknowledge and orient (1-2 sentences)
Reflect the situation back briefly. No advice yet.

### Step 2 — Ask one clarifying question
The single most useful question to understand *what kind* of problem this is. Frame it as a choice between **3-5 concrete options**, not open-ended.

- Option labels are **2-6 words max**, no colons or sub-explanations inside bullets. The question carries context; options are just forks.
- **Mutually exclusive** options that lead down genuinely different paths.
- Concrete labels: "Earn more money", not "financial reasons".
- Cover the realistic space including uncomfortable options ("Scared of failing").
- Always offer an escape: "Not sure yet".

Good format:
> "What's driving this for you right now?
> - Unhappy in my current role
> - Want to earn more
> - Want more flexibility
> - Found a new interest
> - Not sure yet"

Bad format:
> "Tell me more about your situation." ← too open, doesn't reduce the space
> "- Simplicity: I want the easiest setup..." ← option labels never have colons/explanation

### Step 2b — Extract before you ask
If the user's message already contains useful information (constraints, platforms, requirements), summarize it in 1-2 lines, then ask only what's still missing. Don't make them re-answer what they told you.

### Step 3 — Branch based on their answer
Go one level deeper each time. Typical depth: 3-4 levels before actionable steps.
- Level 1 — What kind of problem is this? (motivation, constraint, knowledge gap, fear, resources)
- Level 2 — What's the most important factor? (urgency, risk tolerance, resources)
- Level 3 — What's their current situation / starting point?
- Level 4 (leaf) — Give concrete steps.

### Step 4 — Deliver concrete steps at the leaf
Give 3-6 specific, ordered, immediately-doable action steps (numbered list). Not vague advice.

Good leaf:
> "Based on what you've shared... here's where to start:
> 1. Spend one hour this week writing down what drains vs. energizes you at work.
> 2. Look at 3 job postings... note what skills overlap with yours.
> 3. Reach out to 1-2 people doing those roles for a 20-min conversation.
> 4. Set a decision deadline: apply somewhere within 6 weeks.
> 5. Tell one trusted person about your plan."

Bad leaf:
> "You should network more and update your resume." ← too vague

## When to Branch vs. Go Straight to Steps

**Go straight to steps when:**
- The situation is already specific enough (user answered 3+ questions).
- Remaining branches would all lead to the same advice.
- User says "just tell me what to do".

**Keep branching when:**
- Advice would be meaningfully different depending on the answer.
- You'd be guessing at key constraints (budget, timeline, risk tolerance).
- The problem space still feels large.

**Option counts:** 3 for clear-cut distinctions; 4-5 for genuinely varied space; never more than 5.

## Tone & Format

- Warm but efficient. Short sentences, no jargon.
- Reflect their words back ("burned out", not "fatigued"). Never judge the path chosen.
- Present choices as a short list, not prose. At the leaf, numbered list starting with "1.".
- If the user is clearly distressed (not just confused), pause navigation, acknowledge first.

## Edge Cases

- **Very vague situation** → start even broader: what area of life feels most stuck (work, relationships, health, purpose, finances).
- **User picks "something else"** → ask them to describe briefly, then fit into the next branching level.
- **User wants multiple paths** → finish one path to the leaf, then offer to explore the other path.
- **User is in distress** → pause; acknowledge; ask if they want to talk through feelings or focus on practical next steps.
