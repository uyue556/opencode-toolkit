# Market Sizing & Opportunity Analysis

Calculate market opportunity with three complementary methodologies and produce investor-credible TAM/SAM/SOM reports. Sources: `market-sizing-analysis`, `startup-business-analyst-market-opportunity`, `startup-analyst`.

## The Three-Tier Framework

- **TAM (Total Addressable Market)** — total revenue opportunity at 100% market share. Defines the universe of potential customers. Example: all email-marketing software revenue globally.
- **SAM (Serviceable Available Market)** — portion of TAM targetable with your current product/service, after geographic/segment/capability constraints.
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)** — realistic share achievable in 3–5 years given competition and resources. Used for financial projections and fundraising.

## Three Methodologies — When to Use Each

| Method | When | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Top-down** | Mature markets with research reports | TAM = category size; SAM = TAM × geo% × segment%; SOM = SAM × capture (2–5%) |
| **Bottom-up** | B2B, niche, specific segments (most credible for investors) | TAM = Σ(segment size × annual revenue/customer); SOM = SAM × penetration (yr 3–5) |
| **Value theory** | New categories, disruptive innovations | Price = value created × WTP% (10–30%); TAM = potential customers × price |

**Rule:** lead with bottom-up; use top-down only to validate (results should be within 30%). A TAM unsupported by data, or a TAM < $1B for a VC-backed startup, are both red flags.

### Bottom-up by business model

- **SaaS/B2B**: TAM = Σ(companies in segment × ACV × (1 + expansion rate)).
- **Marketplace**: TAM = category GMV × take rate.
- **Consumer**: TAM = users × ARPU × purchase frequency.
- **B2B services**: TAM = target companies × average deal size × deals/year.

### Value theory process

1. Identify the problem; quantify its current cost (time/money/inefficiency).
2. Value per customer = problem cost × % solved by solution.
3. Price = value × WTP% (typically 10–30% of value created).
4. TAM = total potential customers × price.

## Step-by-Step Sizing

1. **Define the market** — problem, target customer, category, geography, time horizon.
2. **Gather data** — industry reports (Gartner/Forrester/IDC), government stats (Census, BLS), public filings, CRM/sales data, customer interviews. Cite every source with date.
3. **Calculate TAM** with the chosen methodology.
4. **Narrow to SAM** with explicit filters: geographic, product capability, customer requirements, distribution access, regulatory. `SAM = TAM × (% matching all filters)`.
5. **Estimate SOM** — conservative: 2% (yr 3) to 5% (yr 5) of SAM for new entrants. Consider competitive intensity, funding, GTM effectiveness, time.
6. **Validate & triangulate** — compare methodologies (within 30%), check against public-company revenues, sanity-check customer counts and pricing, review with experts.
7. **Document & present** — show methodology, sources, assumptions; structure for audience.

## Data Sources (curated)

- **Premium research**: Gartner ($5K–50K), Forrester ($3K–30K), IDC ($4K–40K); McKinsey insights free.
- **Accessible**: Statista ($39/mo), CB Insights, PitchBook, Grand View Research.
- **Government (free)**: US Census, BLS, SEC EDGAR (10-K segment revenue), Data.gov, OECD, World Bank, Eurostat.
- **B2B counts**: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, ZoomInfo, Crunchbase, BuiltWith.
- **Consumer**: Euromonitor, Nielsen, Mintel.
- **Survey/primary**: SurveyMonkey, Typeform, Qualtrics, Respondent.io (B2B interviews), UserTesting, Google Surveys.
- **Emergency (<2 hrs)**: Statista → public-company 10-Ks → LinkedIn counting → Scholar → bottom-up calc → triangulate.

**Data-quality checklist:** authority, methodology, recency (<2 yr), consistency (multiple sources agree), relevance (matches your market definition).

**Cost-effective approach:** buy 1–2 key reports, use free sources to triangulate, supplement with interviews, cite the mix.

## Report Structure (investor-ready market opportunity)

1. **Executive summary** — opportunity in one paragraph; TAM/SAM/SOM headline numbers.
2. **Market definition** — problem, target profile, geography, horizon.
3. **Bottom-up analysis** — segment breakdown, sizing with sources, formulas, assumptions.
4. **Top-down validation** — category size, filters applied, comparison.
5. **SAM calculation** — filters with rationale, formula, segment-level.
6. **SOM projection** — share assumptions, yr 3 & yr 5, customer-count and revenue implications.
7. **Market growth** — CAGR, growth drivers, 5-year evolution.
8. **Validation & sanity checks** — public-company comparisons, customer-count validation, competitive context.
9. **Investment thesis** — assessment, key positives/risks, venture-scale potential, next steps.

**Do:** start bottom-up, triangulate, cite all sources, document assumptions, be conservative on SOM, compare to public benchmarks.
**Don't:** rely solely on top-down, cherry-pick optimistic data, claim >10% SOM without justification, mix methodologies, skip validation.

**Tips:** sizing takes 30–60 min for thorough analysis; update annually; conservative estimates build investor credibility.
