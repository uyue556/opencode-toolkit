## 1. Mechanism-First Copywriting

Copy converts when it matches the reader's awareness stage, mirrors their lived language, lowers cognitive resistance, and makes the desired choice feel like the natural next step. Style without mechanism does not change belief — label the psychological job each block is doing.

**Execution steps:**
1. **Anchor on the audience's state.** Start from what the reader already believes, fears, and wants.
2. **Translate the job into progress.** Turn the job-to-be-done into a concrete before/after promise; people respond to progress, not feature lists.
3. **Choose the dominant mechanism.** Problem agitation, proof, identity, belonging, relief, or aspiration — decide which route the trust stage calls for, then stay consistent.
4. **Mirror the voice of the customer.** Use the reader's own words for the problem and the desired outcome (self-relevance increases persuasion).
5. **Add proof at the resistance point.** Place evidence exactly where skepticism peaks, not only at the end.
6. **Close with a low-friction next step.** Make the CTA feel like a continuation of the reader's intent; autonomy-preserving prompts beat pressure.

## 2. Context Gathering

Before writing, establish (ask if missing):
1. **The Target Human** — who they are, their job-to-be-done, and their awareness stage.
2. **The Objective** — what belief, feeling, or action must change.
3. **The Output** — ad, landing page, product description, email, or script.
4. **Constraints** — brand voice, length, channel, and ethical limits.

## 3. Decision Matrix

**By awareness stage:**
- Unaware → problem-led copy, high clarity, low jargon.
- Problem-aware → intensify consequences; define the problem precisely.
- Solution-aware → compare approaches; frame differentiation.
- Product-aware → lead with proof, specifics, and preempted objections.
- Most aware → compress; make the CTA frictionless.

**By emotional state:**
- Anxious → safety, certainty, support.
- Frustrated → relief and speed.
- Aspirational → identity, status, progress.
- Skeptical → proof, transparency, specificity.

**By category trust:**
- Low trust → more evidence, less flourish.
- Moderate → blend emotion and proof.
- High trust → move into vivid desire language.

## 4. Failure Modes & Ethics

- **Pretty copy, no mechanism** → label each block's psychological job.
- **Emotional appeals where proof is needed** → match proof density to the awareness stage.
- **Overstated claims** → specificity, bounds, and honesty; credibility collapses when promise exceeds reality.

**Ethical line:** persuasion ends and manipulation begins when copy tries to bypass informed choice by distorting reality or inventing urgency. Never cross it. Truthful persuasion, verifiable claims, freedom to decide.

## 5. Content Marketing Pipeline

Strategic plan, not one-off posts:

1. **Analyze audience** and define objectives + KPIs.
2. **Research competition** and find content gaps.
3. **Develop a content strategy** with themes, pillar pages, and clusters (see `seo-content.md` for the cluster mechanics).
4. **Create optimized content** across formats.
5. **Plan distribution** across channels and platforms.
6. **Implement tracking** (GA4, Search Console, platform analytics).
7. **Optimize from data** with A/B tests on headlines, CTAs, formats.
8. **Scale winners** through repurposing and automation.
9. **Report** with insights, not vanity metrics.
10. **Re-plan** from learnings and trends.

Repurpose one strong piece across blog, social captions, email, and video. Prioritize evergreen content and refresh it on a schedule (see refresh matrix in `seo-content.md`).

## 6. Internal Communications

For progress/status/newsletter/FAQ/incident comms inside a company:

- **Identify the communication type** before writing: 3P updates (Progress, Plans, Problems), company newsletter, FAQ answers, status reports, leadership updates, project updates, incident reports.
- Match format and tone to that type; if the type doesn't fit a known pattern, ask what format is expected.
- Be direct, specific, honest about problems — internal readers value candor and completion over polish.
